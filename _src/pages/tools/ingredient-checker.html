Simplified guidance based on 7 CFR 205.605–205.606. Some conditions (source, processing method, commercial availability) need certifier verification. This is not legal or regulatory advice.

How organic ingredient rules work

In a product labeled “Organic”, at least 95% of ingredients (excluding salt and water) must be certified organic. The remaining 5% may only come from the National List: approved non-agricultural substances such as baking soda or pectin, plus specific non-organic agricultural ingredients that aren't commercially available in organic form.

Why is “natural flavor” conditional?
Natural flavors are allowed only if they're produced without synthetic solvents, carriers or preservatives. Certifiers also require organic flavors when they're commercially available.
Is carrageenan allowed in organic?
Yes, it's currently on the National List. Some brands avoid it because of consumer concerns, so we flag it as conditional.
