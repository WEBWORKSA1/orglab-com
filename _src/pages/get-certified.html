The process

The 10 steps to organic certification

Based on the USDA National Organic Program process. Canada, the EU and others follow a similar path.

  1. Confirm eligibility & transition

    Land must be free of prohibited substances for 36 months before harvest. Livestock have species-specific transition rules.

  2. Pick your standards & markets

    Selling to the US, Canada, EU or all three? Choose standards before choosing a certifier.

  3. Choose an accredited certifier

    Compare fees, inspector availability in your region, turnaround and scope.

  4. Write your Organic System Plan (OSP)

    Practices, inputs, seeds, pest and soil management, buffers, recordkeeping and a field or facility map.

  5. Submit the application & fees

    Application fees usually run $300–$500. Include land history and input documentation.

  6. Initial review

    The certifier checks your OSP for completeness and compliance and may request clarifications.

  7. On-site inspection

    An inspector verifies your practices, records, storage, labels and audit trail. Budget $400–$1,500+.

  8. Final review & decision

    The certifier reviews the inspection report. Minor non-compliances can be corrected.

  9. Receive your certificate

    Use the USDA Organic seal and your certifier's name on labels. Get label artwork approved first.

  10. Renew annually & claim cost-share

    Update your OSP, get inspected every year, and claim up to 75% of costs back through cost-share.

FAQ

Certification questions

How long does organic certification take?
For land already free of prohibited substances for 36 months, expect 3–6 months from application to certificate. Processors and handlers can often be certified in 2–4 months. Expedited review can shorten this.
Do small farms need to be certified?
In the US, operations selling $5,000 or less in organic products a year are exempt from certification. They must still follow the standards and can't use the USDA seal.
Is OrgLab's matching service really free?
Yes, for applicants. Partner certifiers, consultants and labs may pay OrgLab a referral or listing fee. This never affects our editorial content or test results.
Can I get certified in Canada or the EU and sell in the US?
The US has organic equivalency arrangements with several markets, including Canada, the EU, the UK, Japan, Korea, Switzerland and Taiwan, with specific conditions. Many certifiers can certify you to multiple standards in a single inspection.
What's the biggest reason applications get delayed?
An incomplete Organic System Plan: missing input documentation, a missing land history or a weak audit trail. Our free Roadmap includes a checklist to avoid this.
