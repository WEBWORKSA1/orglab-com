Organic Gardening for Beginners

OLBy OrgLab Research DeskLast reviewed {{TODAY}}10 min read

The 5 rules

  • Feed the soil, not the plant.
  • Right plant, right place, right time.
  • Diversity beats pests.
  • Mulch everything.
  • Observe weekly and act early.

1. Choose your site

Most vegetables need at least 6–8 hours of direct sun. Start small: a 4×8 ft raised bed managed well beats a big plot you can't keep up with. Put the garden close to water and close to your kitchen door.

2. Build living soil

Get a soil test from your local extension service; many cost $15–$30. Aim for a pH of 6.0–7.0. Add 2–3 inches of finished compost every year and avoid deep tilling, which disrupts fungal networks and soil structure.

3. Make compost

Mix greens (nitrogen) and browns (carbon) to a C:N ratio near 25–35:1, and keep the pile as moist as a wrung-out sponge. Our Compost Mixer does the maths.

4. Plan by frost dates

Cool-season crops like peas, spinach and lettuce go in before your last frost. Warm-season crops like tomatoes, peppers and squash go in after it. Get dates for 30 crops from the Planting Calendar.

5. Manage pests organically

  • Prevent: use resistant varieties, rotate crops and space plants for airflow.
  • Attract allies: flowering herbs such as dill, alyssum and yarrow draw in ladybugs, lacewings and parasitic wasps.
  • Block: row covers stop cabbage moths and flea beetles.
  • Treat last: insecticidal soap, neem, Bt (for caterpillars) and spinosad, used as the label directs.

6. Seeds

Choose certified organic or untreated seed, and open-pollinated varieties if you want to save your own. Keep a garden journal. Next year's garden starts with this year's notes.
