The four USDA organic label tiers

LabelOrganic contentUSDA seal allowed?
100% OrganicAll ingredients and processing aids organicYes
Organic≥95% (excluding salt & water)Yes
Made with Organic ___≥70%No
Organic ingredients listed only<70%No (only in the ingredient list)

Every organic label must name the certifying agent (“Certified organic by…”). If a product claims to be organic but names no certifier and isn't from an exempt small operation, be skeptical.

“Natural” is not organic

For meat and poultry, “natural” means minimally processed with no artificial ingredients. For most other foods there's no binding definition, no inspection and no farming standard. It scores 1/10 in our Label Decoder.

Beyond organic: the stricter labels

  • Regenerative Organic Certified: requires organic certification, then adds soil health, animal welfare and social fairness requirements.
  • Demeter Biodynamic: a whole-farm system with biodiversity set-asides and on-farm fertility.
  • Real Organic Project: an add-on requiring soil-grown crops and pasture-based livestock.

Labels that aren't about organic at all

Non-GMO Project Verified is about GMO avoidance only; synthetic pesticides are still allowed. Fair Trade covers labour and pricing. Both are useful, but neither replaces organic.
