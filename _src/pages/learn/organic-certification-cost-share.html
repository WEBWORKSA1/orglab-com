Organic Certification Cost-Share: How to Get Up to 75% Back

OLBy OrgLab Research DeskLast reviewed {{TODAY}}6 min read
Program terms, funding and deadlines are set each program year. Confirm the current rules with your state department of agriculture or local USDA Farm Service Agency (FSA) office.

What it is

The Organic Certification Cost Share Program (OCCSP), run by USDA's Farm Service Agency, reimburses certified organic producers and handlers for part of their certification costs. In recent program years the formula has been 75% of eligible costs, up to $750 per certification scope.

Eligible scopes

  • Crops
  • Wild crops
  • Livestock
  • Handling
  • State Organic Program fees (where applicable, e.g., California)

A farm certified for crops and livestock that also handles its own product could claim up to $2,250 across three scopes.

Eligible costs

Application fees, inspection costs, fees related to equivalency arrangements, annual certification fees, and travel and per-diem costs charged by inspectors. Not eligible: equipment, materials, late fees, and consulting or training.

How to apply

  1. Get certified (or recertified) within the program year.
  2. Keep your certifier's invoices and proof of payment.
  3. Apply through your local FSA office or a participating state agency before the deadline, which usually falls shortly after the program year ends.
